The first challenge of this problem is deciding how to separate the digits so that they can be handled individually.
Numbers and integers are primitive values that can't be iterated, and we can't change the data type of the input to something like a string or an array.

A number can be viewed as a sum of its individual parts.
Let's try to view the integer by decomposing it into a sum of its place-value parts.

<PlaceValue />

We can see that the sum of the digits is actually the sum of the item in its place-value divided by the power of that digit.

So `1` can be immediately added since it's in the ones place.
`20` is in the tens place, so we divided it by 10 and can add it to `1`.
The same holds true for the `300` in the hundreds place where we divide it by 100 and `6000` in the thousands place where we divide it by 1000.
Furthermore, each step can be viewed as divided by increments of `10`, so we can decompose the division by viewing `/ 1000` as `/ 10 / 10 / 10`.

What we are doing is converting each digit to its representation in the ones place by dividing by factors of 10.
