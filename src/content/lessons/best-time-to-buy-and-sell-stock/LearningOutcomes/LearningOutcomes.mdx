This problem uses a concept known as a greedy algorithm which optimizes our solution by finding the best answer locally without needing to compare all the values like in the brute force solution.
If we make the best choice at any point, we will have the overall optimal answer once we reach the end of the input.

You may not explicitly recognize that that, "Hey, I can use a greedy approach to solve this problem."
Usually as you problem solve your way to the optimal solution and realize the values you need to track, a greedy algorithm reveals itself (unless you've just seen a problem before).
You recognize that you can find the optimal answer by going through the input once and making the optimal decision based on the available data.

If you do recognize a greedy approach, it's essential to prove that it always yields the best solution.
In more complex problems, there are instances where you can get stuck in a locally optimal answer without yielding a globally optimal solution.
