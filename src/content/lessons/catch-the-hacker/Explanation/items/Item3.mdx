Sorting can be assumed to be an <BigO.nlogn /> which is immediately better than our brute force solution.
By sorting, we would group all email addresses together that are the same.
We could then iterate through the sorted array and count email addresses.
When the email address sequence changes, we would compare this total to the max and then start counting the new addresses.
This iteration would cost <BigO.n /> time complexity but is ignored since the sort complexity is larger.

By sorting our list, we would get a time complexity of <BigO.nlogn /> and space complexity of <BigO.constant />.

Typically we are fine with adding additional space complexity if we can improve the time complexity.
This tradeoff happens by introducing another data structure that has faster operations than our array.
