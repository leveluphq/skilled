We walk through the nested loops and check if the items at `i` and `j` add up to our target.
If they do, we add that pair to our output.

> Notice that the inner loop starts at `j = i + 1`.
> This ensures that we don't check the same indexes more than once.
> You may also think that improves our time complexity since we are reducing the total number of computations.
> However, remember that we drop constants when calculating Big O, and by starting at `j + 1` each iteration, we are performing ~`n^2 / 2` operations which is still <BigO.n2 />.
> We drop constants because as the input size grows large, constants lose meaning between different Big O complexities.

It is worth noting that the space complexity is constant <BigO.constant /> because we don't create any new data structures
aside from the `results` array which may or may not count against your space complexity, depending on what the interviewer specifies.

The issue is that we repeat the same work over and over to look for the items that sum to the target.
To restate that, arrays have an <BigO.n /> search time,
and so for all `n` items in the array, we perform an additional <BigO.n /> search.

A brute force solution can almost always be optimized.
