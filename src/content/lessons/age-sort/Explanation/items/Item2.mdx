Now that we have buckets, we just need to figure out how to map that to a sorted array.

Imagine we have 5 orders:

<Container>

```javascript
 Customer | Age
----------------
    A     |  35
    B     |  20
    C     |  35
    D     |  40
    E     |  35
```

</Container>

Our output would be `[20, 35, 35, 35, 40]`.
We have three orders that match to a customer at 35 years old,
but in the output it doesn't really matter which customer it is associated to.
The 35 associated to Customer A is no different than the 35 associated with Customer C or E.
We just know that we need to account for 35 three times in the output.

All we need to know is the total number of customers in each age bucket.
From that, we can construct our sorted output array.
This fixed-range of possible values is exactly how <ConceptModal content={<React.Fragment><h2>Bucket Sort</h2><BucketSort /></React.Fragment>}>bucket sort</ConceptModal> improves over the standard sorting time complexity.

So for our input above, we create buckets with counts like the following:

<Container>

```javascript
 Age | Count
------------
 20  |   1
 35  |   3
 40  |   1

// Construct the solution based on the counts
solution = [20, 35, 35, 35, 40]
```

</Container>

We can also visualize a simple example of bucketing:

<Image src="/images/bucket-sort.gif" maxWidth="500px" alt="Counting values into buckets allows the sorted output to be reconstructed from bucket frequencies." width={800} height={649} />

Our solution algorithm will consist of two simple steps:

1. Count the number of times every age occurs
2. Iterate through all the ages and insert it into the solution array based on the total count

With this, we only perform <BigO.n /> operations which reduces our overall time complexity from <BigO.nlogn /> to <BigO.n />.
